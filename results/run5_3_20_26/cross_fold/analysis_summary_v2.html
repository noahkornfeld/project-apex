⚠️ Technical Flags & Concerns
High Q-value spike of 35,153 at the very end of Fold 2 Ep3
At 23:20:31 — exactly 25 seconds before Fold 2's training completed and the checkpoint was saved — a single alarm fired: |q1_mean| = 35153.59 > 100. Every logged update in the preceding hour showed Q-values of 13–15, which is fully stabilized. This isolated spike occurred on the last mini-batch of training, meaning the final saved model weights may reflect a momentarily extreme network state rather than the stabilized policy.

Fold 2 produced the run's highest Sortino (6.59) and a strong CAGR (+32.9%). These results may be valid, but there is no intermediate checkpoint to compare against and rule out that the final checkpoint is an outlier. This should be investigated before presenting Fold 2 results as definitive.
High Single-week returns of 25–29% when QQQ is flat or negative
15 weeks across 8 folds showed portfolio returns above 15%. The most extreme examples: +27.4% (Fold 1, QQQ −0.9%), +25.7% (Fold 2, QQQ flat), +28.9% (Fold 4, QQQ −1.1%), +27.2% (Fold 5, QQQ +0.6%). These require the agent to have nearly all NAV concentrated in 1–2 stocks that happened to have the best week in the universe.

The majority of these spike weeks fall on low-liquidity calendar dates: New Year's week (3 occurrences), week of July 4th (4 occurrences), Thanksgiving week. In a real fund these weeks would have thin volume and materially higher slippage than the cost model assumes. If these 15 weeks were capped at a realistic 10%, average Sharpe drops from 1.11 to 0.97 and average Sortino from 2.63 to 1.52 — still strong, but more defensible numbers.
Medium turnover_mean: NaN across all 8 folds — metric computation bug
Every fold's oos_metrics.json shows turnover_mean: NaN. The root cause is a one-line bug: the call to compute_all_metrics() in run_full_apex.py hardcodes turnover=None despite the w_exec_hist array being available right there in scope. Turnover can be trivially computed from consecutive weight differences. rank_ic is also NaN across all folds (requires per-asset forward returns, which are not being passed).

This matters for the presentation: without confirmed OOS turnover figures, you cannot show that the strategy's cost assumptions were met in practice. Any audience member who asks "what's the weekly turnover?" will not have an answer from the official metrics.
Medium Bellman clip pinned at ceiling in Folds 6, 7, 8 — all of Ep3
In the three most recent folds (2020-present), Q-values were pinned at the 30.0 clip ceiling for most or all of the third training episode. TD error dropped to 0.3–0.8 (normal is 1.0–2.5), indicating the network received very little gradient signal during its final episode of learning. In Fold 6, 67% of logged Ep3 updates had Q-values above 27. The clip ceiling of 30 was calibrated to arrest Fold 2's known divergence — it is too restrictive for post-2020 market data where the agent's natural value estimates are higher.
Medium Extreme positive skewness across most folds — standard risk metrics understate tail risk
Fold 1 skewness = 8.21, kurtosis = 75.1. All folds except Fold 7 have right-skewed, fat-tailed return distributions, driven primarily by the spike weeks. A Sharpe ratio computed on a distribution with kurtosis of 75 is not interpretable the same way as one computed on a near-normal distribution. Fold 7 (bear market) is the only fold with negative skew and normal-ish kurtosis — its results are the most statistically trustworthy. The others are better interpreted with CVaR or conditional metrics, not just Sharpe.
Low Fold 3 (2014–2015) persistent underperformance — structural, not a bug
Fold 3 is the only fold where the agent clearly underperformed QQQ on both Sharpe (0.60 vs 0.88) and Sortino (0.78 vs 0.99). Training was clean — 54.4% hit rate (highest of any fold), no divergence, no clip fires. This is a structural issue: 2014–2015 was an unusually low-volatility steady uptrend where simple momentum (holding QQQ) is hard to beat with active stock selection. The agent has no explicit cross-sectional momentum feature to identify which individual stocks are trending strongest relative to peers.

1
Raise bellman_clip_high from 30 → 35
1-line config · overnight re-run

Problem: The clip ceiling of 30 is too tight for post-2020 market data. Folds 6, 7, and 8 had their Q-values pinned at the ceiling for the entire third episode, dropping TD error to 0.3–0.8. The agent effectively stopped learning in its final episode for the three most recent and most presentable folds.

Why it matters: Folds 6, 7, and 8 cover 2020-to-present. These are the periods your audience cares most about — they're recent and live-adjacent. If those agents converged prematurely due to a suppressed gradient, their policies are suboptimal and the results are a lower bound on what's achievable.

Fix: Change bellman_clip_high: 30.0 → 35.0 in master_config.yaml. The Fold 2 divergence peaked at raw Q~333 internally — well above 35 — so the clip still fires and still arrests it. Low risk. Kick off Run 5 overnight.
2
Fix turnover_mean: NaN — pass w_exec_hist to metrics
2-line code fix · no re-run needed

Problem: turnover=None is hardcoded in the compute_all_metrics() call in run_full_apex.py. The w_exec_hist array is already being built and is in scope immediately above the call.

Why it matters: Without confirmed turnover figures you cannot show that the cost model was applied correctly. Any presentation audience member who asks "what's the weekly turnover?" currently has no answer from the official metrics. It also directly validates (or challenges) the spike week results — if turnover spikes to 80%+ on those weeks, that's informative.

Fix: Compute turnover_arr = [0.5 * np.sum(np.abs(w_exec_hist[t] - w_exec_hist[t-1])) for t in range(1, len(w_exec_hist))] and pass it to compute_all_metrics(turnover=np.array(turnover_arr)). Then recompute metrics for all 8 existing folds without a full re-run.
3
Add a per-asset position cap (~20% max weight)
1–2 hrs in ConstraintProjector · requires re-run

Problem: The agent appears to concentrate 70–90%+ NAV into 1–2 stocks on specific weeks, producing returns of 25–29% — plausible mechanically but not realistic in a fund context, especially in thin-volume holiday periods. This concentration inflates Sortino and Sharpe through a small number of high-skew outlier weeks. Capping returns at 10%/week reduces average Sharpe from 1.11 → 0.97 and average Sortino from 2.63 → 1.52.

Why it matters: 0.97 / 1.52 are still excellent results and more defensible in a presentation than numbers that depend on freak concentrated weeks. A 20% single-stock cap is also a standard institutional risk management constraint — framing it as intentional risk management strengthens the narrative rather than weakening it.

Fix: Add max_single_weight: 0.20 to the constraint projector config and enforce it as a per-asset clip after the portfolio optimization step. With 110 possible assets the agent still has full degrees of freedom; this only prevents the pathological all-in cases. Add to Run 6 (after Run 5 evaluates the clip change).
4
Save intermediate checkpoints (end of each episode)
30 min code change · adds ~3 files per fold

Problem: Only the final episode checkpoint is saved. The Fold 2 Q-spike of 35,153 fired on the last mini-batch of training — the checkpoint captures that momentary state and there is no earlier save to compare against. Additionally, some folds may converge best at Ep2 rather than Ep3; with only final checkpoints, there is no way to test this.

Why it matters: Fold 2 produced the run's highest metrics and also has the most suspicious training artifact (the late spike). Being able to compare Ep2 vs Ep3 checkpoints for Fold 2 would either confirm the results or explain an anomaly. For the presentation, saying "we select the best checkpoint per fold" is a stronger methodology than "we always use the final one."

Fix: In run_full_apex.py, save a checkpoint at the end of each episode to checkpoints/fold_N/model_ep{E}.pt in addition to the final. Costs ~90MB per fold in disk space and zero training runtime.
5
Add cross-sectional momentum feature to address Fold 3
1–2 weeks · requires x_panel rebuild

Problem: Fold 3 (2014–2015) is the only fold where the agent clearly underperformed QQQ. The agent currently has no feature that tells it which individual stocks are trending strongest relative to each other within the NDX universe. In low-volatility steady uptrends, cross-sectional momentum (buy the relative leaders) is one of the few reliable signals.

Why it matters: Improving Fold 3 would bring the Sharpe beat-rate from 5/8 to 6/8, which strengthens the presentation narrative. However this is a per-asset feature (goes in the x_panel, not the g_panel), meaning it requires a full x_panel rebuild across all 8 folds — a larger undertaking than the other items on this list.

Fix: Add each stock's 12-week return minus the cross-sectional median as a per-asset feature in features/per_asset_features.py, then rebuild all x_panels. Only attempt if items #1–3 are implemented and Run 5/6 leave time for a Run 7 before April 11.
