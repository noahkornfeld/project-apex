What is Project Apex?

🤖

An AI Portfolio Manager

Apex is a reinforcement learning system that manages a portfolio of up to 100 Nasdaq-100 stocks. It decides weekly what to buy, hold, or sell — on its own, without human input.

📈

Goal: Beat QQQ

QQQ is the standard Nasdaq-100 ETF. Apex's entire purpose is to outperform QQQ on a risk-adjusted basis — earning more return per unit of downside risk.

🎓

Learns from 22 Years of History

Apex trained on market data from 2004 to 2026, covering financial crises, bull markets, COVID, and everything in between. The hard lessons are baked in.

🔒

Realistic Constraints

No short selling. No more than 20% in one stock. Real trading cost simulation. Sector caps. This is not a theoretical backtest — it models actual portfolio management.

The Core Analogy

Imagine hiring a portfolio manager fresh out of school. You sit them in a room with 22 years of market history and say: "Make decisions every week. When you do well, you get a bonus. When you lose money or take too much risk, you get penalized." After millions of simulated weeks, that manager has developed real intuition about what works.


That is Apex. Except instead of a person, it is a neural network trained through a process called reinforcement learning. The "bonus and penalty" system is called the reward function. The weekly decision-making is called the policy.


Unlike most investing algorithms that follow rigid rules ("buy when RSI drops below 30"), Apex learned its own rules from experience. It discovered what patterns matter without being told explicitly.

The One-Sentence Pitch

"Project Apex is a reinforcement learning system that acts as an autonomous Nasdaq-100 portfolio manager, trained across 22 years of market history and tested rigorously on out-of-sample data to outperform QQQ on a risk-adjusted basis."

The SAC Algorithm: Actor vs. Critic

SAC (Soft Actor-Critic) is the reinforcement learning algorithm that trains the neural network. Here's what each part does:

The Actor (Decision-Maker)

Looks at the current market state and outputs portfolio weights. This is the policy — the actual brain making trading decisions. It wants to act in ways the Critic will rate highly.

The Critic (Evaluator)

Estimates how good a given action is expected to be — not just now, but into the future. Two Critics are used simultaneously to prevent overconfidence. They learn by comparing predictions to actual outcomes.

The "Soft" Part: Entropy Bonus

The "soft" in SAC means the agent is also rewarded for being unpredictable and exploratory. This prevents it from getting stuck in a rigid strategy that works in calm markets but breaks down in volatility. The entropy level (called alpha) is tuned automatically during training.

The 8 Folds: Walk-Forward Testing

Why Does This Matter?

The number one problem with AI trading systems is overfitting: the model memorizes historical patterns but fails in live markets. Walk-forward testing is the most rigorous defense against this. Each of the 8 folds trains on older data and tests on a completely new, unseen future period. If a strategy only works because it "memorized" history, the out-of-sample test will expose it immediately.

In-sample (training): The data the model learned from. Like studying for an exam.
Out-of-sample (testing): Data the model never saw. Like taking the actual exam blind.
Embargo (4 weeks): A gap between train and test prevents "leakage" of recent signals.
Fold Training Period Test Period Market Regime Key Challenge
Fold 1 2005 – 2009 2010 – 2011 Recovery Can the agent adapt after training through a crash?
Fold 2 2006 – 2011 2012 – 2013 Bull Market Full crisis + recovery in training; calm bull market test
Fold 3 2008 – 2013 2014 – 2015 Choppy / Low Vol Low volatility choppy market — hard for momentum strategies
Fold 4 2010 – 2015 2016 – 2017 Post-Recovery Bull Steady grind upward; risk of over-trading
Fold 5 2012 – 2017 2018 – 2019 Volatility Spike Includes Q4 2018 crash (-20%); recovery in 2019
Fold 6 2014 – 2019 2020 – 2021 COVID / Recovery Fastest crash in history followed by biggest bull run
Fold 7 2016 – 2021 2022 – 2023 Bear Market QQQ fell 33%; hardest period for any long-only strategy
Fold 8 2018 – 2023 2024 – present Recent Bull AI-driven rally; concentration in mega-cap tech

Key Metrics Explained

These are the numbers you'll need to discuss fluently with the panel. Know what each one measures and why it matters.

CAGR (Compound Annual Growth Rate)
Total Return ^ (1/years) - 1
The annualized return — how much the portfolio grew per year on average, compounded. Think of it as the single number that tells you "how fast did the portfolio grow?"
Apex target: Beat QQQ's CAGR after all costs
Sharpe Ratio
Return / Total Volatility
Risk-adjusted return using all volatility (both up and down moves count as "risk"). Higher is better. A Sharpe of 1.0+ is considered good; most hedge funds target 0.5-1.5.
Apex: 1.18  |  QQQ: 1.01
Sortino Ratio
Return / Downside Volatility only
Like the Sharpe Ratio but smarter for investors: it only penalizes for bad (downside) volatility. Going up fast is not a risk — only going down is. This is the most important metric for Apex.
Apex: 2.45  |  QQQ: 0.92 (strong outperformance)
Maximum Drawdown (MaxDD)
Peak value to Trough value / Peak value
The worst peak-to-trough loss the portfolio experienced. If you started at $100, dropped to $70, then recovered — that's a -30% max drawdown. Investors hate large drawdowns because they shake people out of the strategy.
Apex: ~-21.4% (comparable to QQQ)
Beta to QQQ
Cov(Apex returns, QQQ returns) / Var(QQQ)
How much Apex moves when QQQ moves. Beta = 1.0 means they move together. Beta = 0.8 means Apex moves 20% less than QQQ (more defensive). Apex targets 0.8-1.2 — actively managed but not detached from the market.
Target range: 0.8 to 1.2
Information Ratio (IR)
Excess Return / Tracking Error
How much extra return Apex earns per unit of "deviation" from QQQ. A high IR means the system generates consistent alpha (outperformance) rather than just getting lucky on a few big bets.
Separate from Sharpe — do not confuse them
Turnover
Average weekly one-way trade size / portfolio value
How much of the portfolio is traded each week. Lower turnover means lower transaction costs. Apex targets 5-15% weekly turnover — active enough to capture opportunities, not so active that costs eat returns.
Target: 5% to 15% weekly
Cost Drag
Total trading costs / Gross return
The percentage of raw returns lost to transaction costs (commission, bid-ask spread, market impact). Should stay under 50% of generated alpha — otherwise trading activity is destroying more value than the strategy creates.
Target: less than 50% of gross alpha

Sharpe vs. Sortino: Why the Difference Matters

Sharpe (all volatility)

If Apex gains 8% in a week, Sharpe counts that as risk. But rallying is not a problem for investors — it's exactly what they want. Sharpe can unfairly penalize strategies that have strong upside moves.

Sortino (downside only)

Sortino only penalizes losses. A Sortino of 2.45 vs QQQ's 0.92 means Apex generates returns with far less of the bad kind of risk. This is the headline number for this presentation.

The Reward Function: How the AI Learns

The reward function is the rulebook that tells the AI what "good" looks like each week. It has five components — three positive (you want more of these) and two negative (penalties for bad behavior). Understanding this shows you really understand what Apex is optimizing for.

Reward =
  + (excess return / market volatility)   ← beat QQQ, scaled for market conditions
  + downside protection bonus        ← reward for avoiding big losses
  + diversification bonus             ← reward for holding multiple positions
  - transaction cost penalty         ← discourages over-trading
  - portfolio volatility penalty       ← discourages excessive risk-taking
Excess Return / Market Vol
The primary signal. Did we beat QQQ this week? Dividing by market volatility means the reward is bigger for the same outperformance when markets are calm — so the agent learns to be selective rather than leveraged.
Downside Protection Bonus
When the agent avoids large losses, it gets a bonus. This is why the Sortino ratio is so strong — the reward function explicitly teaches the AI to care more about preventing drawdowns than maximizing raw returns.
Diversification Bonus
Rewards the agent for holding many positions rather than concentrating in 1-2 stocks. Prevents the degenerate strategy of putting everything in NVIDIA and calling it a portfolio.
Transaction Cost Penalty
Every trade costs money. The penalty here teaches the agent to trade only when the expected gain justifies the cost. Without this, the agent would over-trade and costs would eat the alpha.
Portfolio Volatility Penalty
Controlled by lambda_slow = 0.50. Penalizes the agent for taking too much risk. This is why Apex has a better Sortino than Sharpe — it's literally been taught to hate downside risk more than it loves returns.

The Bellman Equation (What the Critic Learns)

The Critic estimates not just the immediate reward but the total expected future reward from any given state. It does this using the Bellman equation, which says: "The value of being here now equals the reward I get now, plus the discounted value of where I end up next."

Q(state, action) = reward_now + 0.975 × Q(next_state, next_action)

The 0.975 is the discount factor (gamma). It means the agent values a reward 4 weeks from now at 97.5% of its face value — very patient, appropriate for a long-horizon investor. The Bellman clip (±30) prevents the Q-values from exploding during volatile periods like 2008-09.
